# Aeon — Brand Voice Guidelines (seed version)

*Synthesized from six uploaded documents: Aeon Sign's email marketing templates (general, Amazon DSP/AFP, FedEx ISP, and the finalized cold-email campaign), Aeon Miles' email-and-calling sequence, and the Aeon Miles cold-calling script. High confidence for Aeon Sign and Aeon Miles — both are grounded in real, in-use material. No source material yet for Aeon RecruitPro or Aeon Scheduler; see Open Questions.*

## We Are

- **Consultative, peer-framed** — "we're not looking to replace your team," "our co-founder is a former DSP owner" — positioned as someone who understands the operator's world, not a vendor cold-calling a list
- **Discovery-first, not closing-first** — the explicit goal of a first call is understanding how the prospect works today, not pitching
- **Permission-asking** — "would you be open to...", "if that's okay with you" — gives the prospect control, lowers resistance
- **Benefit-led, revealed progressively** — each touch in a sequence adds one new angle (workload, then financial impact, then an objection answered, then a low-risk trial) rather than repeating the same pitch
- **Comfortable proposing a small first step** — "start with one area," free-tier or trial framing, rather than asking for a full commitment upfront
- **Concise** — short paragraphs, one clear CTA per email, no feature-catalog dumps

## We Are Not

- **Not hype-driven** — no "revolutionary," "game-changing," "unlock." Concrete language only.
- **Not pushy on price** — "don't immediately give the entire pricing sheet" is explicit guidance in the existing calling script; pricing follows understanding the prospect's actual need
- **Not guilt-tripping in win-back/nurture** — "might become relevant later in the year, or should I leave it here?" — a real, low-pressure exit, not a manufactured urgency play
- **Not aggressive past the defined breakup point** — sequences have an explicit, honest close-out step ("should I close the loop?"), not indefinite badgering

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach, first touch | Curiosity-driven, short, one soft CTA — introduces the category of help without a feature list |
| Follow-up emails | Reference the prior touch briefly, add exactly one new angle — never just repeat the first email |
| Calls | Discovery-first; ask permission before diving in; if they push on price, redirect to understanding their situation first; ask the key question, then stop talking |
| Objection handling | Normalize the objection ("that's great to hear — a lot of DSPs we work with also started with in-house teams"), then offer a low-commitment next step |
| Breakup/close-out | Give an explicit choice (close the loop / send an overview / redirect to the right person) — never just go silent, never guilt |
| Win-back / nurture | Acknowledge the time gap directly, ask if timing has changed, no pressure |

## Recurring product-story anchors

- **Aeon Sign**: "Send → Collect → Track → Store" — use as the one-line positioning anchor whenever the product needs summarizing in a single phrase
- **Aeon Miles**: opens with a workload question ("how much of the day-to-day work is still on your team?") before describing what Aeon Miles does — makes the prospect articulate their own pain before hearing the pitch

## Signature style

First name + product, not a full corporate block in the body: `Best,\nEric\nAeon Miles`. Keep it light in the body; full contact details belong in an actual signature block, not repeated in every email's prose.
